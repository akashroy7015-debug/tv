Best Image Formats for Websites

PNG vs JPG vs WebP — pick the right one for speed and quality.

The image format you choose affects how fast your site loads and how sharp it looks. Pick the wrong one and pages get heavy or pictures look blurry. Here's a simple way to decide.

JPG — best for photographs

JPG (JPEG) uses lossy compression that's excellent for photos with lots of colors and gradients. Files are small, which keeps pages fast. The downside: it doesn't support transparency, and very high compression can add visible artifacts. Use JPG for photos, hero banners, and thumbnails.

PNG — best for graphics & transparency

PNG is lossless and supports transparency, so it's perfect for logos, icons, screenshots, and anything with sharp edges or text. The trade-off is larger files than JPG for photos. Use PNG for logos, UI graphics, and images that need a transparent background.

WebP — the modern all-rounder

WebP supports both lossy and lossless modes and transparency, usually at 25–35% smaller file sizes than JPG or PNG at similar quality. Every modern browser supports it. Use WebP as your default for the web when you can, falling back to JPG/PNG for older tools that don't read it.

Quick rules of thumb

  • Photo, no transparency: WebP (or JPG for maximum compatibility).
  • Logo/icon/needs transparency: WebP or PNG.
  • Screenshots with text: PNG or WebP (lossless).
  • Print or editing master: keep a PNG/original; export web copies separately.

Performance tips

  • Resize images to the size they're actually displayed — don't ship a 4000px photo into a 600px slot.
  • Compress before uploading; quality around 80–85% is usually indistinguishable and much smaller.
  • Serve WebP where possible for the biggest speed win.
