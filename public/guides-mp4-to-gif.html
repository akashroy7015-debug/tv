How to Convert MP4 to GIF

Turn a short clip into a looping animated GIF that plays anywhere.

A GIF is the quickest way to share a few seconds of action — a reaction, a product demo, a bug reproduction, a how-to step — in places that do not autoplay video. GIFs loop automatically, need no player, and embed in chat apps, wikis, forums and documents. This guide shows how to turn an MP4 into a GIF, and how to keep the file from getting huge.

When should you use a GIF?

Use a GIF for short, silent, looping clips — ideally under about 10 seconds. GIFs have no sound and use a limited colour palette, so they are perfect for screen recordings and reaction clips but a poor choice for full videos. For anything longer or with audio, keep it as MP4. Keeping the file size down

GIF is an old format that stores each frame as an image, so file size grows fast. To keep your GIF small and snappy: trim the clip to just the seconds you need before converting, prefer shorter clips, and remember that simple, flat-colour content (screen recordings, UI) compresses far better than busy, high-motion footage. A 30-second 1080p clip can easily become a 20 MB GIF, which is why short is always better.

Is it private?

Frequently asked questions

Will the GIF have sound? No — the GIF format does not support audio. If you need sound, keep the clip as MP4.

Why is my GIF bigger than the MP4? Video codecs are far more efficient than GIF; a short GIF can be larger than the original video. Trim the clip to reduce size.

What length works best? Aim for under 10 seconds for smooth, shareable GIFs.
