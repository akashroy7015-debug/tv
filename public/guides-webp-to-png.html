How to Convert WebP to PNG

Turn web-downloaded WebP images into the universally editable PNG format.

You saved an image from a website, and now your photo editor or document tool refuses to open it — because it downloaded as a .webp file. WebP is everywhere on the modern web, but it is not yet accepted everywhere offline. Converting it to PNG makes it open in any editor, keeps full quality, and preserves transparency. Here is how, and when it makes sense.

What is WebP?

WebP is an image format developed by Google to make web pages load faster. It can be either lossy (like JPG) or lossless (like PNG), and it usually produces files 25–35% smaller than the equivalent JPG or PNG. That is why so many sites serve images as WebP. The downside: some desktop apps, older Office versions, and certain upload forms still do not recognise it.

Why convert WebP to PNG?

  • Editor support: PNG opens in every image editor, ever.
  • Transparency preserved: if your WebP has a transparent background, PNG keeps it intact.
  • Lossless quality: PNG never adds compression artefacts, so the image stays crisp.
  • Document & print workflows: tools that reject WebP almost always accept PNG.

Step by step

  • Open the FileMorph converter and pick the Image tab.
  • Drop in your .webp file or click browse.
  • Choose Convert to → PNG.
  • Click Convert and download the PNG.

PNG vs JPG as the target

Convert to PNG when you need transparency or pixel-perfect quality — logos, icons, screenshots, graphics with text. If the WebP is a photograph and you only care about a smaller, widely-supported file, JPG may be the better target. Our PNG vs JPG guide breaks down the decision in detail.

Is it private?

Completely. FileMorph converts images in your browser, so the file never leaves your device and is never uploaded to a server. You can convert sensitive graphics or client work without sending anything to the cloud.

Troubleshooting

  • Animated WebP: only the first frame is captured when converting to a still PNG.
  • File got bigger: that is expected — PNG is lossless, so it is often larger than the compressed WebP. That is the cost of perfect quality.

Frequently asked questions

Will I lose quality? No. PNG is lossless, so converting from WebP to PNG does not add any new compression artefacts.

Does PNG keep the transparent background? Yes — transparency is fully preserved.

Why is the PNG larger than the WebP? WebP compresses more aggressively; PNG trades size for perfect fidelity.
