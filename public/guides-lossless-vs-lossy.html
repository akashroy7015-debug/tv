Lossless vs Lossy Compression

Why some files shrink more — and what you give up.

Compression makes files smaller. The big question is whether it throws any data away.

Lossy compression

Lossy compression discards information your eyes or ears are unlikely to miss, achieving much smaller files. JPG, MP3, AAC and most video use it. The trade-off: quality drops a little each time you re-save, and heavy compression can show artifacts.

Lossless compression

Lossless compression keeps every bit of the original — it just stores it more efficiently. PNG, FLAC and ZIP are lossless. Files are larger than lossy, but quality is perfect and unchanged no matter how many times you save.

Which should you use?

  • Photos for the web: lossy (JPG/WebP) — small and good enough.
  • Logos, screenshots, editing masters: lossless (PNG).
  • Casual music: lossy (MP3/AAC). Archiving music: lossless (FLAC/WAV).

Rule of thumb: use lossy for sharing, lossless for keeping the original safe.
