How to Convert HEIC to JPG

A complete, private way to make iPhone photos open everywhere.

If you have ever emailed an iPhone photo to someone on Windows, or tried to upload one to an older website, you have probably hit the "this file type isn't supported" wall. That is almost always because the photo is a HEIC file. Converting it to JPG solves the problem in seconds while keeping your picture looking great. This guide explains what HEIC is, why it causes friction, and exactly how to convert it without uploading your photos to a stranger's server.

What is HEIC, and why do iPhones use it?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is Apple's default photo format since iOS 11. It is built on the HEIF standard and uses modern HEVC compression, which means a HEIC photo is roughly half the file size of the same photo as a JPG at similar quality. That is fantastic for your phone's storage — but the trade-off is compatibility. Many Windows apps, web forms, content management systems and older photo editors still cannot open HEIC, so the file that looks perfect on your iPhone becomes unusable the moment it leaves Apple's ecosystem.

Why convert HEIC to JPG?

  • Universal compatibility: JPG opens on virtually every device, browser, and app ever made.
  • Uploads and forms: job portals, government sites and marketplaces that reject HEIC will accept JPG.
  • Editing: nearly every photo editor handles JPG natively, with no plugins.
  • Sharing: friends and colleagues on Windows or Android can open it instantly.

Step by step: convert HEIC to JPG

  • Open the FileMorph converter and select the Image tab.
  • Drag in your .heic file, or click browse to pick it from your device.
  • Set Convert to → JPG.
  • Adjust the quality slider if you want a smaller file or maximum detail.
  • Click Convert, then download your new JPG.

Choosing the right quality

JPG is a "lossy" format, meaning it discards some data to save space. For photos you intend to print or archive, keep quality at 90% or higher so detail and gradients stay clean. For pictures you are posting online or sending in a chat, 70–80% produces a much smaller file that still looks excellent on screen. There is rarely a reason to go below 60% unless file size is critical.

Is it private?

Yes. With FileMorph, image conversions run entirely inside your browser using your own device's processing power. Your photo is never uploaded to a server, never stored, and never seen by us. That makes it the right choice for personal pictures — family photos, documents, ID scans — that you would rather not hand to an anonymous online tool.

Troubleshooting

  • "Decoding is slow": very large or 48-megapixel ProRAW-style HEICs take longer; give it a few seconds.
  • Colours look slightly different: HEIC can store a wider colour range; converting to JPG maps it to the standard sRGB space, which is normal.
  • Live Photos: only the still frame is converted — the short video portion is dropped, which is expected for a still image format.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting to JPG reduce quality? Slightly, because JPG is lossy — but at 90%+ the difference is invisible to the eye for everyday photos.

Can I convert many HEIC files at once? Convert them one after another; because it all happens locally, there are no upload waits between files.

Should I convert to PNG instead? Only if you need lossless quality or transparency — for normal photos JPG is smaller and the better choice. See our PNG vs JPG guide.
