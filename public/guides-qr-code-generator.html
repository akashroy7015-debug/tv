How to Make a QR Code

Turn any link or text into a scannable QR code — free, in your browser.

QR codes are everywhere — on menus, posters, business cards, packaging and Wi-Fi sign-ins. They're just a fast way to hand someone a link or piece of text without them typing anything: point a phone camera, tap the notification, done. This guide explains what they are and how to make one in seconds with FileMorph's free QR code generator.

What is a QR code?

A QR ("Quick Response") code is a square barcode that stores data — most commonly a URL, but also plain text, contact details, or Wi-Fi credentials. Modern phone cameras read them natively, so there's no app to install. The pattern includes error-correction, which is why a QR code still scans even if part of it is scratched or a logo covers the middle.

PNG or SVG — which should you download?

Use PNG for screens, social posts and quick sharing. Use SVG for print (posters, packaging, signage) — because SVG is a vector, it stays perfectly sharp at any size, from a business card to a billboard.

Tips for QR codes that always scan

  • Keep good contrast: dark code on a light background scans best. Avoid light-on-light.
  • Don't print too small: aim for at least 2×2 cm; bigger for posters seen from a distance.
  • Leave a quiet zone: keep clear space (margin) around the code so the camera can find it.
  • Test before you print with a couple of different phones.

What can a QR code link to?

Anything with a URL: your website, a menu PDF, a Google review page, a WhatsApp chat, an app-store listing, a payment link, or a social profile. You can also encode plain text or contact info. For a menu or document, host the file online first and point the QR code at that link.

Frequently asked questions

Do QR codes expire? A static QR code (like the ones here) never expires — it permanently encodes whatever you put in. Only the destination page can change or go offline.

Is it free? Yes — generate and download as many as you want, no sign-up.

Is my link private? Yes. It's encoded on your device; nothing is sent to a server.
