How to Convert WAV to MP3

Shrink huge uncompressed audio into a compact, shareable file.

WAV files sound great because they are uncompressed — but that also makes them enormous, often 10 times larger than the same recording as an MP3. If you need to email a voice memo, upload a podcast, or just free up space, converting WAV to MP3 is the answer. This guide covers the quality trade-off and how to convert.

WAV vs MP3

WAV stores the full, raw audio waveform with no compression, so a few minutes of stereo audio can be tens of megabytes. MP3 uses lossy compression that removes sound information your ears are least likely to notice, producing a dramatically smaller file. For speech, music demos, and almost all everyday listening, a good-quality MP3 is indistinguishable from the WAV to most people.

Why convert WAV to MP3?

  • Much smaller files: easier to email, upload and store.
  • Universal playback: MP3 plays on every phone, browser and media player.
  • Podcast & web ready: most platforms expect compressed audio like MP3.

How much quality do you lose?

MP3 is lossy, so some data is discarded — but at a standard quality level the result sounds clean for speech and very good for music. Keep the original WAV if you plan to do further editing or mastering, since repeatedly re-compressing lossy audio degrades it. Convert to MP3 for the final, shareable version. To understand the trade-off, see our lossless vs lossy guide.

Is it private?

Your audio is used only to perform the conversion and is not retained. FileMorph handles audio with an in-browser converter where possible and a secure service for larger files.

Frequently asked questions

Is MP3 good enough for music? For listening, yes — most people cannot tell a quality MP3 from the original. For studio editing, keep the WAV.

How much smaller will the file be? Typically around a tenth of the WAV size.

Can I go back to WAV later? You can convert MP3 to WAV, but the discarded detail does not return — keep your original WAV if you may need it.
