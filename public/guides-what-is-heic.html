What Is HEIC?

The space-saving photo format behind every modern iPhone picture.

If you own an iPhone, most of your photos are quietly saved as HEIC files. It is a smart, modern format — but it is also the reason your pictures sometimes won't open on a friend's PC. This explainer covers what HEIC actually is, why Apple uses it, and what to do when something can't read it.

The basics

HEIC stands for High Efficiency Image Container. It is Apple's implementation of the HEIF standard, and it stores images compressed with HEVC (the same technology behind efficient 4K video). Apple made it the default in 2017 with iOS 11 to save storage on phones with ever-larger cameras.

Why Apple uses it

  • Half the size: a HEIC is roughly 50% smaller than the same photo as a JPG, at similar quality.
  • Richer images: it supports more colours (higher bit depth) and stores extras like depth maps and Live Photo data.
  • Multiple images in one file: handy for bursts and Live Photos.

The catch: compatibility

HEIC is excellent, but support outside Apple's world is still patchy. Many Windows apps, older photo editors, web upload forms and content systems cannot open it. So a photo that looks perfect on your iPhone can be rejected the instant you try to use it somewhere else — the number-one frustration people have with the format.

What to do about it

You have two options. You can tell your iPhone to shoot in JPG instead (Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible), which trades some storage savings for universal compatibility. Or you can keep shooting in HEIC for the space savings and simply convert photos to JPG or PNG when you need to share them. Our HEIC to JPG guide walks through it — and because FileMorph converts images in your browser, your photos are never uploaded.

HEIC vs JPG, in short

  • HEIC: smaller, richer, but limited support — great for storing on your phone.
  • JPG: larger, universally supported — great for sharing and uploading.

Frequently asked questions

Is HEIC better quality than JPG? At the same file size, generally yes — it is a newer, more efficient format. The downside is purely compatibility.

Will I lose quality converting HEIC to JPG? Only slightly at high quality; for everyday photos the difference is invisible.

Can Windows open HEIC? Recent Windows versions can with an add-on, but many individual apps still can't — converting avoids the hassle.
