What Is an SVG?

The image format that never gets blurry.

SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) stores images as math — points, lines and curves — instead of a grid of pixels. That means it can scale to any size without losing sharpness, from a tiny favicon to a billboard.

Why designers love it

  • Crisp at every resolution and on every screen.
  • Tiny file sizes for logos and icons.
  • Editable as code; can be styled and animated.

When NOT to use SVG

SVG is for flat graphics — logos, icons, illustrations. It's a poor fit for photographs, which are better as JPG/WebP. Some apps also can't import SVG, so you may need a PNG.

Converting SVG

To use an SVG where vectors aren't supported, rasterize it to PNG — see SVG to PNG. You choose the output size, and the result stays crisp at that resolution.
