What Is a Codec?

The piece that actually compresses your audio and video.

A codec (coder-decoder) is the method used to compress and decompress media. H.264, H.265, VP9 and AV1 are video codecs; MP3, AAC and Opus are audio codecs. The codec determines quality and file size.

Codec vs container

People mix these up. A container (like MP4, MKV or MOV) is the "box" that holds the video stream, audio stream and subtitles. The codec is how each stream inside is encoded. An MP4 file usually contains H.264 video and AAC audio, for example.

Why it matters

Two files can both be ".mp4" but use different codecs — which is why one plays fine and another doesn't on the same device. Converting to a widely supported combo (MP4 with H.264 + AAC) fixes most playback problems.

Common pairings

  • MP4: H.264 video + AAC audio — the most compatible.
  • WebM: VP9/AV1 + Opus — open and web-friendly.
  • MKV: can hold almost anything.
