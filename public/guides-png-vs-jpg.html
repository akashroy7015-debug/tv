PNG vs JPG: Which Should You Use?

A plain-English guide to the two most common image formats.

PNG and JPG are the two image formats you meet most often, and picking the right one makes your files look better and download faster. The short version: JPG is for photographs, and PNG is for graphics, logos, screenshots and anything that needs transparency. Here is why, and how to choose with confidence.

How they compress (the key difference)

JPG uses lossy compression: it permanently discards detail your eye is unlikely to notice, which makes photos very small. PNG uses lossless compression: it keeps every pixel exactly, which makes it perfect for sharp edges and flat colour but results in larger files for photos. For more on this, see lossless vs lossy compression.

When to use JPG

  • Photographs and realistic images with smooth colour gradients.
  • When small file size matters more than perfect pixels.
  • Email attachments, galleries, and most social uploads.

When to use PNG

  • Logos, icons and illustrations with crisp lines and solid colours.
  • Anything that needs a transparent background.
  • Screenshots, especially with text — PNG keeps text razor-sharp.
  • Images you will edit repeatedly, since lossless means no quality loss over time.

Why JPG can look "blocky"

Because JPG throws data away, pushing the quality too low produces visible artefacts — blocky squares around edges and muddy text. That is exactly why screenshots and logos should be PNG: those hard edges are where JPG struggles most. For photos at high quality, the artefacts are invisible.

Quick decision table

  • Photo, want it small → JPG
  • Logo / icon / graphic → PNG
  • Needs transparency → PNG
  • Screenshot with text → PNG
  • Print-quality photo → JPG at 90%+ (or PNG if size is no concern)

Frequently asked questions

Is PNG always higher quality than JPG? PNG is lossless, but for photographs a high-quality JPG looks the same to the eye at a fraction of the size.
