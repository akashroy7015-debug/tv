How Image Compression Works

Why a photo can go from 8 MB to 800 KB and still look fine.

Compression makes image files smaller by storing the same picture more cleverly — and, for lossy formats, by dropping detail you won't notice.

Two ways files shrink

  • Lossless (PNG): finds patterns and stores them compactly. Nothing is lost.
  • Lossy (JPG/WebP): removes fine detail and color information the human eye barely registers.

The quality dial

Lossy formats let you choose a quality level. Around 80–85% is usually the sweet spot: big size savings with no visible difference. Push it too low and you'll see blocky "artifacts" around edges.

Practical tips

  • Resize before compressing — don't keep a 4000px image where 800px will show.
  • Use WebP for the smallest web files.
  • Keep a lossless master (PNG/original) and export compressed copies.
