How to Convert SVG to PNG

Turn a scalable vector graphic into a ready-to-use image.

SVG is a brilliant format for logos and icons because it is a vector — it stays razor-sharp at any size. But many tools do not accept SVG: social media uploads, some presentation and document apps, email signatures, and marketplaces often want a plain image. Converting SVG to PNG gives you a universally-supported file while keeping crisp edges and transparency.

Vector vs raster: what changes

An SVG describes shapes with maths, so it can scale infinitely. A PNG is a raster image made of a fixed grid of pixels. When you convert, you "rasterize" the vector at a specific resolution — so the key decision is what size to export. Export at the largest size you will actually use; you can always scale a big PNG down cleanly, but scaling a small one up makes it blurry.

Why convert SVG to PNG?

  • Universal support: PNG works in every app, upload form and platform.
  • Transparency kept: a transparent SVG background carries over to the PNG.
  • Predictable rendering: a PNG looks identical everywhere, with no font or rendering quirks.
  • Social & email ready: places that block SVG (for security) accept PNG.

Step by step

  • Open the FileMorph converter and select the Image tab.
  • Drop in your .svg file.
  • Set Convert to → PNG.
  • Click Convert and download your PNG.

Getting sharp results

Because PNG is fixed-resolution, always rasterize at a generous size — for a logo destined for the web, exporting at 2× the display size keeps it crisp on high-density "retina" screens. If your SVG uses custom fonts, make sure the text is rendered as expected before relying on the PNG.

Is it private?

Yes — SVG-to-PNG runs in your browser, so your graphic never leaves your device. Great for unreleased logos and client branding.

Frequently asked questions

Will the PNG be sharp? Yes, if you export at a large enough size. Remember the PNG no longer scales infinitely like the SVG did.

Does it keep transparency? Yes — a transparent SVG background becomes a transparent PNG.

Can I get the SVG back from the PNG? No — rasterizing is one-way, so keep your original SVG for future edits.
