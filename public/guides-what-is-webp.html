What Is WebP?

The format that makes modern websites load faster — and why it sometimes annoys you.

Ever right-clicked an image online, saved it, and found a .webp file your editor won't open? That is WebP — a modern image format that is brilliant for the web but not yet supported everywhere offline. Here is what it is and how to handle it.

The basics

WebP is an image format created by Google to shrink images for faster-loading web pages. Its trick is flexibility: it can compress in a lossy mode (like JPG) or a lossless mode (like PNG), and it also supports transparency and even animation — combining the best traits of several older formats in one.

Why websites love it

  • Smaller files: typically 25–35% smaller than an equivalent JPG or PNG.
  • Faster pages: smaller images mean quicker loading and better search rankings.
  • One format, many jobs: photos, graphics with transparency, and animations.

The downside

All modern browsers display WebP, but the offline world lags behind. Some desktop editors, older versions of Office, certain upload forms and a few messaging apps still don't accept it. That mismatch is why a file that loads fine on a website becomes awkward the moment you download it.

WebP vs JPG vs PNG

  • vs JPG: WebP makes smaller photos at similar quality, and unlike JPG it can do transparency.
  • vs PNG: WebP's lossless mode produces smaller files than PNG for graphics.
  • Compatibility: JPG and PNG still win for "open anywhere," which is when you convert.

What to do when something won't open a WebP

Convert it. Turn it into a PNG when you need transparency or perfect quality, or a JPG when you just need a small, universally-supported photo. Our WebP to PNG guide covers it step by step, and the conversion runs in your browser so nothing is uploaded.

Frequently asked questions

Is WebP better than JPG and PNG? Technically it is more efficient, but "better" depends on support — for the open web WebP wins, for compatibility JPG/PNG win.

Does WebP support transparency? Yes, like PNG — and it can be smaller.

Why did my download become a WebP? The site served the image as WebP for speed; your browser saved it in that format.
